Morphologically conservative taxa

Time: Devonian, Carboniferous, Permian, Triassic, Jurassic, Cretaceous

Region: Global distribution (USA, China, Germany, UK, etc.)

Formation: Multiple localities worldwide

Coloration Data

The scales of numerous fossil fishes retain discernible in-life pigmentation patterns. Mesozoic insects — such as giant lacewings of the family Kalligrammatidae — frequently preserve conspicuous wing spots, bands, and eyespots, while ancient wasps, flies, and beetles exhibit structural iridescence. Lacewings of the genus Lichenipolystoechotes bore intricate branching wing patterns specialized for lichen mimesis. In fossil crinoids, organic aromatic pigments identical to those found in living species have been isolated, demonstrating that prehistoric forms exhibited vibrant, diverse coloration comparable to their modern counterparts. Furthermore, original color banding, zigzags, and spots have been widely reconstructed across numerous fossil mollusks and brachiopods.

Method: Scanning electron microscopy (SEM), geochemical analysis of pigment biomarkers, and visual examination of preserved pigment impressions.

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

This database contains information on animals that lived before the Cenozoic era, whose coloration is known to scientists to some extent. A brief overview is provided for each, along with links to original research and PDF files if available in open access. Reconstructions, fossils, and size comparisons are also included.

The website features representatives of every period from the Cambrian to the Cretaceous. Their coloration has been studied using a wide range of methods: from the well-known melanosome analysis of feathered dinosaurs to direct observation of the original colors of birds in Burmese amber, the analysis of diffraction gratings on the setae of Cambrian polychaetes, and the prediction of color patterns based on the scale morphology of Edmontosaurus mummies from the United States.
