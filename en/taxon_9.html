Borealopelta markmitchelli

Time: Early Cretaceous (~110 million years ago)

Region: Alberta, Canada

Formation: Clearwater Formation

Coloration Data

In Brown et al. (2017), specimen TMP 2011.033.0001 was investigated. A total of 147 samples were taken across various parts of the fossil, allowing researchers to analyze the head and most of the dorsal armor. Organic residues chemically consistent with pheomelanin were detected, preserved within the scales and the keratinous sheaths of the osteoderms. The distribution of this pigment demonstrates countershading camouflage: the skin and armor of Borealopelta markmitchelli were brownish (most likely a reddish-brown tone), whereas the venter and prominent lateral spines were lighter than the rest of the dorsal armor.

Method: Time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (ToF-SIMS) and pyrolysis gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (Py-GC-MS).

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

This database contains information on animals that lived before the Cenozoic era, whose coloration is known to scientists to some extent. A brief overview is provided for each, along with links to original research and PDF files if available in open access. Reconstructions, fossils, and size comparisons are also included.

The website features representatives of every period from the Cambrian to the Cretaceous. Their coloration has been studied using a wide range of methods: from the well-known melanosome analysis of feathered dinosaurs to direct observation of the original colors of birds in Burmese amber, the analysis of diffraction gratings on the setae of Cambrian polychaetes, and the prediction of color patterns based on the scale morphology of Edmontosaurus mummies from the United States.
