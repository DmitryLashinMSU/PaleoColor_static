Anchiornis huxleyi

Time: Late Jurassic (~160 million years ago)

Region: Liaoning Province, China

Formation: Tiaojishan Formation

Coloration Data

In Li et al. (2010), 29 plumage regions of specimen BMNHC PH828 were examined, reconstructing the animal's coloration through melanosome analysis, excluding the tail and non-feathered parts of the body (see artistic reconstruction). Subsequently, in a study by Lindgren et al. (2015), researchers found no pheomelanin — the pigment responsible for reddish coloration — in the crest of Anchiornis huxleyi specimen YFGP-T5199. However, the authors note that due to differences in size and slight anatomical variations, this specimen may represent a different ontogenetic stage, reflect sexual dimorphism, or even belong to a distinct, closely related species of feathered dinosaur.

Method: Scanning electron microscopy (SEM) to examine melanosome morphology and density, followed by comparison with those of extant birds. Mass spectrometry for molecular composition analysis.

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

This database contains information on animals that lived before the Cenozoic era, whose coloration is known to scientists to some extent. A brief overview is provided for each, along with links to original research and PDF files if available in open access. Reconstructions, fossils, and size comparisons are also included.

The website features representatives of every period from the Cambrian to the Cretaceous. Their coloration has been studied using a wide range of methods: from the well-known melanosome analysis of feathered dinosaurs to direct observation of the original colors of birds in Burmese amber, the analysis of diffraction gratings on the setae of Cambrian polychaetes, and the prediction of color patterns based on the scale morphology of Edmontosaurus mummies from the United States.
