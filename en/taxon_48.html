Enantiornithes indet. in Burmese amber (hatchlings)

Time: Mid-Cretaceous (~99 million years ago)

Region: Kachin State (Hukawng Valley), Myanmar

Formation: Burmese amber

Coloration Data

Isolated avian wings and an exceptionally preserved, articulated enantiornithine hatchling have been recovered entombed in Burmese amber. Visual analysis of the macroscopic pigmentation preserved in the isolated wing specimens demonstrated countershading: the remiges were brown with darker apical tips, and the dorsal contour feathers were deep brown, whereas the ventral side of the wing and the down feathers were pale or white. In addition, a distinct pale spot was present at the base of the alula on both wings. In the near-complete hatchling specimen HPG-15-1, darkly pigmented, bristle-like integumentary structures resembling primitive protofeathers were identified along the legs and tail. Its flight feathers were predominantly brown, accented by pale transverse bars and pale tips.

Method: Direct visual and optical microscopic analysis of preserved pigments under transmitted and reflected light through the transparent amber matrix.

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

This database contains information on animals that lived before the Cenozoic era, whose coloration is known to scientists to some extent. A brief overview is provided for each, along with links to original research and PDF files if available in open access. Reconstructions, fossils, and size comparisons are also included.

The website features representatives of every period from the Cambrian to the Cretaceous. Their coloration has been studied using a wide range of methods: from the well-known melanosome analysis of feathered dinosaurs to direct observation of the original colors of birds in Burmese amber, the analysis of diffraction gratings on the setae of Cambrian polychaetes, and the prediction of color patterns based on the scale morphology of Edmontosaurus mummies from the United States.
