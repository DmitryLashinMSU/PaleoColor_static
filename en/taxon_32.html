Eggshells

Time: Cretaceous

Region: USA, China, Mongolia, Argentina, France

Formation: Multiple localities across several countries

Coloration Data

In Wiemann et al. (2015), the eggshell coloration of the oviraptorid Heyuannia huangi was analyzed by detecting molecular pigment biomarkers — protoporphyrin and biliverdin. The authors suggest that the eggs of Heyuannia were likely olive-green (if uniformly colored) or speckled with brown spots on a blue-green ground (if maculated). Subsequently, Wiemann et al. (2018) conducted a broad-scale survey of fossil and extant eggshells spanning from the Cretaceous to the present day. Mesozoic taxa analyzed included Maiasaura peeblesorum, Deinonychus antirrhopus, and an indeterminate troodontid from the USA; an enantiornithine bird, a microtroodontid, and a troodontid from Mongolia; a saltasaurid from Argentina; a titanosaur from France; and Heyuannia huangi from China. The study demonstrated that all sampled Cretaceous ornithischian and sauropod eggshells lacked pigments (and were thus unpigmented/white). In contrast, eggshells across the sampled Cretaceous eumaniraptorans were pigmented. The sole exception among the maniraptorans was the North American troodontid, in which pigments were absent — a condition interpreted by the authors as a secondary evolutionary loss of eggshell pigmentation.

Method: High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC), Electrospray Ionization Quadrupole Time-of-Flight Mass Spectrometry (ESI-Q-TOF-MS), and high-resolution Raman spectroscopy.

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

This database contains information on animals that lived before the Cenozoic era, whose coloration is known to scientists to some extent. A brief overview is provided for each, along with links to original research and PDF files if available in open access. Reconstructions, fossils, and size comparisons are also included.

The website features representatives of every period from the Cambrian to the Cretaceous. Their coloration has been studied using a wide range of methods: from the well-known melanosome analysis of feathered dinosaurs to direct observation of the original colors of birds in Burmese amber, the analysis of diffraction gratings on the setae of Cambrian polychaetes, and the prediction of color patterns based on the scale morphology of Edmontosaurus mummies from the United States.
