Stenopterygius sp.

Time: Early Jurassic (~180 million years ago)

Region: Baden-Württemberg, Germany

Formation: Posidonia Shale

Coloration Data

Ichthyosaur coloration has been investigated in several key studies. Initially, Lindgren et al. (2014) examined the tail fluke of an indeterminate ichthyosaur (specimen YORYM 1993.338). Based on this material and other fossils with complete soft-tissue outlines, the authors inferred that ichthyosaurs were uniformly dark-colored. However, a later and more detailed investigation by Lindgren et al. (2018) on specimen MH 432 (Stenopterygius sp.) revealed that pigmentation was differentially distributed, strongly indicating countershading (a dark dorsal surface and a pale ventral surface). Additionally, the researchers identified dendritic melanophores — cellular structures that in modern reptiles are responsible for physiological color change via pigment translocation.

Method: Integrated molecular and cellular analyses (including SEM, SRXTM, SRS-XRF, and ToF-SIMS).

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

This database contains information on animals that lived before the Cenozoic era, whose coloration is known to scientists to some extent. A brief overview is provided for each, along with links to original research and PDF files if available in open access. Reconstructions, fossils, and size comparisons are also included.

The website features representatives of every period from the Cambrian to the Cretaceous. Their coloration has been studied using a wide range of methods: from the well-known melanosome analysis of feathered dinosaurs to direct observation of the original colors of birds in Burmese amber, the analysis of diffraction gratings on the setae of Cambrian polychaetes, and the prediction of color patterns based on the scale morphology of Edmontosaurus mummies from the United States.
