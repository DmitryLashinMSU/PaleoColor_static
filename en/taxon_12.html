Diplodocus sp. (juvenile)

Time: Late Jurassic (~150–145 million years ago)

Region: Montana, USA

Formation: Morrison Formation, Mother's Day Quarry

Coloration Data

In Gallagher et al. (2025), several skin specimens (CMC VP 20857, CMC VP 20858, CMC VP 15915, CMC VP 10660, VP 10662, and VP 10659) belonging to a juvenile Diplodocus were examined. The precise anatomical placement of these epidermal fragments on the body has not been determined. Elongated microbodies interpreted as fossilized melanosomes were discovered within the scales. Their dimensions fall within the size range of pheomelanosomes (brown/reddish pigments) of extant birds, as well as the melanosomes of the black caiman. The diversity of these structures suggests that the dinosaur's skin possessed an intricate color pattern, indicating that juvenile diplodocids were likely spotted or mottled. However, because fossil melanosomes can undergo diagenetic alterations in shape over geological time, the exact hues of this patterning remain unresolved.

Method: Scanning electron microscopy (SEM).

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

This database contains information on animals that lived before the Cenozoic era, whose coloration is known to scientists to some extent. A brief overview is provided for each, along with links to original research and PDF files if available in open access. Reconstructions, fossils, and size comparisons are also included.

The website features representatives of every period from the Cambrian to the Cretaceous. Their coloration has been studied using a wide range of methods: from the well-known melanosome analysis of feathered dinosaurs to direct observation of the original colors of birds in Burmese amber, the analysis of diffraction gratings on the setae of Cambrian polychaetes, and the prediction of color patterns based on the scale morphology of Edmontosaurus mummies from the United States.
