Anurognathidae

Time: Middle–Late Jurassic (~164–160 million years ago)

Region: Inner Mongolia and Liaoning Province, China

Formation: Yanliao Biota — Daohugou Beds and Tiaojishan Formation

Coloration Data

Scanning electron microscopy of two anurognathid pterosaur specimens (NJU-57003 and CAGS-Z070) revealed densely packed microbodies within their pycnofibres. The vast majority of these were ovoid, consistent with pheomelanosomes (responsible for reddish/rufous coloration), while less frequent rod-shaped bodies were interpreted as eumelanosomes (black pigmentation). Spectroscopic profiles of the fossil material aligned much more closely with pheomelanin-rich signatures than with eumelanin, indicating that these animals were predominantly reddish-brown in life.

Method: Scanning electron microscopy (SEM), Fourier-Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR), and Energy-Dispersive X-ray Spectroscopy (EDX).

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

Coloration of pre-Cenozoic extinct animals

This database contains information on animals that lived before the Cenozoic era, whose coloration is known to scientists to some extent. A brief overview is provided for each, along with links to original research and PDF files if available in open access. Reconstructions, fossils, and size comparisons are also included.

The website features representatives of every period from the Cambrian to the Cretaceous. Their coloration has been studied using a wide range of methods: from the well-known melanosome analysis of feathered dinosaurs to direct observation of the original colors of birds in Burmese amber, the analysis of diffraction gratings on the setae of Cambrian polychaetes, and the prediction of color patterns based on the scale morphology of Edmontosaurus mummies from the United States.
